# Learning Path

The objective is not to memorize jargon. It is to understand each concept well enough to explain how it changes a trade decision and how software could measure it.

## Stage 1 — Market mechanics

Learn: spot versus futures, long versus short, contracts, leverage, margin, liquidation, funding, fees, bid, ask, spread, slippage, order book, and order types.

Completion check: explain the full lifecycle of a leveraged short, including every way it can lose money.

## Stage 2 — Reading price

Learn: candlesticks, timeframe, trend, range, swing high/low, HH/HL/LH/LL, support, resistance, breakout, rejection, volatility, volume, and liquidity.

Completion check: describe the same chart without using emotional words such as “looks strong” or “feels weak.”

## Stage 3 — Indicators

Learn: EMA, SMA, ATR, RSI, volume measures, and open interest. Treat indicators as transformations of market data, not predictions.

Completion check: state each indicator’s inputs, output, lag, and failure modes.

## Stage 4 — Strategy construction

Define: eligible markets, market regime, setup, trigger, invalidation, entry, stop, target, sizing, management, exit, and no-trade rules.

Completion check: two independent people examining identical data reach the same decision.

## Stage 5 — Risk and evidence

Define portfolio limits; account for fees, funding, slippage, gaps, outages, and correlated positions; then backtest and paper-test.

Completion check: the maximum intended loss is known before entry and failure scenarios have explicit responses.

## Stage 6 — Automation

Translate approved rules into data requirements, formulas, states, events, safeguards, logs, and acceptance tests.

Completion check: software never has to interpret words such as “near,” “large,” “recent,” or “strong.”
